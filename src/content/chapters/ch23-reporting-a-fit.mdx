A fit is judged by what is reported with it. A structure without its settings, its spread and its checks can't be tested by anyone else, and often not by you a month later. The fitting procedure ends with a report of 22 items, every one filled. Its paper gives the reason: "Every item has to be filled, so a fit cannot be judged from a number the agent chose to show." The same holds for a person. This chapter rewrites the 22 items for someone working in X-Ray Calc and fills them in for CoC4.

## Why a report

The author's own fits show why. When the procedure was tested against them, the project files of the author's fits carried no χ² at all. The structure was there. The number that says how well it fits the curve was not.

A report makes three things visible. The first is how the curve was prepared and which settings shaped the fit. The second is how far repeated runs agree. The third is which checks the fit passed and which it failed. Each chapter from <a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch10-is-this-curve-worth-fitting#is-this-curve-worth-fitting`}>Chapter 10: Is this curve worth fitting?</a> to <a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch18-judging-a-fit#writing-the-verdict`}>Chapter 18: Writing the verdict</a> produced some of these items. The report collects them in a fixed order.

## What the program keeps, and what it doesn't

**File > Save project** (Ctrl+S) writes an `.xrcx` file. The file keeps:

- the structure: every value, its **Min** and **Max**, and the boxes that pair or freeze it;
- the profiles and tables that a Polynomial or Irregular fit created;
- the settings: λ, Δθ, **R min**, the mode, the polynomial order, **Population**, **Iterations**, the χ² weighting, **Solve scale in χ²** and its **Window**, **Smooth**;
- the data as prepared for the fit, after normalizing, smoothing and trimming.

It also keeps a record of the last fit: χ², the plain χ², the solved scale factor, the date, the fit time, the device, the program version and the random seed. A new fit replaces the record. To repeat a fit, type its seed into the **Seed** box of the **Advanced fitting settings** dialog (the **Advanced Settings** button). The same seed repeats the fit exactly only on the same device. The project doesn't hold the raw measurement.

<InXRayCalc>

Right after a fit you accept:

1. **Result > Export fit results...** writes the fit record as a `.json` file: the program version, the date, the fit time, the device and the seed; χ² and the plain χ²; the solved scale and whether it was clamped; the χ² weighting and **R min**; every setting as the fit ran; and the structure with its limits, its values in every period and its polynomial coefficients.
2. **Result > Fit report ...**, then **Copy as text**, puts the order table, the edge, the fringes, the residual by band and the parameters near a limit on the clipboard. Paste them into your report.
3. **File > Save project As ...** (F2) saves the project under a name of its own.
4. Read the **χ²**, **plain χ²** and **scale** fields of the Chart Info bar into the report. The scale reads "solved x…", or "solved x… at bound" when the window clamped it.

Keep the raw measurement file next to the project.

</InXRayCalc>

<Warning title="one record per project">The project keeps the record of its last fit only. A new fit replaces it. So export the fit results, and save the project under a new name, before you start the next run.</Warning>

The record describes the fit as it ran. A setting you change after the fit doesn't enter it.

## Writing the numbers

**The reported structure is the best run.** Report the run with the lowest χ², with the spread of each parameter over the runs beside it. Don't report a mean over the runs. The procedure's reason: "a mean over seeds is a structure no seed found" (§8.4). The spread is the sample standard deviation over the runs. It says how well the fit fixes a parameter. It is not an error bar of the measurement (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch16-fit-settings#several-runs-and-their-spread`}>Chapter 16: Several runs, and their spread</a>).

**An undetermined parameter gets no number.** When the spread is larger than the precision you would claim, write "undetermined" (§10.7; <a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch18-judging-a-fit#what-the-runs-dont-agree-on`}>Chapter 18: What the runs don't agree on</a>). When two layers trade thickness, report their sum and say that the split is undetermined (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch18-judging-a-fit#two-layers-as-a-sum`}>Chapter 18: Two layers as a sum</a>).

**A density is a free parameter.** List every density with the words "free parameter, not a result" (§13, item 16). A density on its bulk ceiling is reported at the ceiling. Give the reason the limit stayed there (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch20-density-a-free-parameter#reporting-a-density`}>Chapter 20: Reporting a density</a>).

**σ is reported as fitted.** Don't say which interface of a period is the rougher one, or on which side an interlayer sits, unless repeated fits of each assignment agree (§10.14; <a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch18-judging-a-fit#which-interface-is-rougher`}>Chapter 18: Which interface is rougher?</a>).

**Every limit travels with its calibration.** A verdict limit was set on certain curves, under certain settings. When your fit lies outside them, the verdict says so (§10.5).

**Say what you chose.** When the floored points outnumber the visible orders, **R min** is "a floor you have chosen rather than one you have measured" (§1.3; <a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch11-reading-the-curve#the-plateau-and-the-background`}>Chapter 11: The plateau and the background</a>). Report the scale factor and its window with every fit (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch14-preparing-the-data#letting-the-fit-solve-the-scale`}>Chapter 14: Letting the fit solve the scale</a>). Report both fits of the mode test. The fit you didn't keep belongs in the report too (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch17-periodic-or-polynomial#decide-on-the-drift-not-on-χ`}>Chapter 17: Decide on the drift, not on χ²</a>).

**A single film** reports its thickness, the total of the layers on the substrate, the deposition rate, and σ and ρ as undetermined (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch21-single-films#reporting-a-film`}>Chapter 21: Reporting a film</a>). **A graded stack** reports what its runs agree on: a profile in Polynomial mode, the set of periods and the total in Irregular mode (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch22-graded-multilayers-and-supermirrors#what-the-fit-can-tell-you-about-each-layer`}>Chapter 22: What the fit can tell you about each layer</a>).

<Remember title="a fail is a result">A fit that fails a check is reported with the fail and its cause. Don't loosen a limit to make it pass. Don't leave the failed check out either (<a href={`${import.meta.env.BASE_URL}/ch19-when-the-fit-will-not-converge#when-to-stop`}>Chapter 19: When to stop</a>).</Remember>

## Keeping the files

Keep these together, under names that say what they hold:

- **the raw measurement**, as it came from the instrument. The project keeps only the prepared curve.
- **the project of every run** (**File > Save project As ...**), with the run's number in its name.
- **the exported fit results** of the reported run (`.json`).
- **the report**, with the Fit report text pasted in.
- **a picture of the chart**, from **Result > Save as graphics ...** (BMP, EMF or WMF) or **Result > Copy as BMP**.

<Warning title="automatic saving">**File > Settings ...** can save every fit automatically (**Automatically save results to output folder after fitting**), as `<name>-fitted.xrcx` in the **Fitting Output** folder. With no folder set, the file goes next to the project. A project that was never saved sends it to the **Default Project's Folder**.</Warning>

## What this means when you fit

- **Export the fit results before the next fit.** The project keeps the record of its last fit only.
- **Fill all 22 items.** Write "none" where nothing was done.
- **Report the best run, with the spread of each parameter.** Never a mean over runs.
- **Write "undetermined" without a number** where the runs don't agree. Report a sum where only the sum is fixed.
- **Call every density a free parameter,** and give its ceiling when it sits there.
- **State the calibration of every limit,** and say where the fit lies outside it.
- **Report a fail with its cause.** Don't change a limit to make the fit pass.
- **Keep the raw file, every run's project and the exported results together.**
