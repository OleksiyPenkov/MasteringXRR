Part 4 · Chapter 22

Graded Multilayers and Supermirrors

In This Chapter

  • Why a period that changes with depth spreads a Bragg order
  • Three ways to describe a graded stack, and what each costs
  • A period that changes with depth, fitted in Polynomial mode
  • A supermirror fitted in Irregular mode
  • What the fit can tell you about each layer, and what it can't

A periodic multilayer repeats one period. A graded multilayer changes its period with depth. Sometimes the change is an accident of the deposition. Sometimes it is the design. A supermirror carries the design to the end: its periods are all different, so that it reflects a wide band of angles. This chapter fits one stack of each kind in X-Ray Calc, using two curves from the paper that describes X-Ray Calc 3.

Graded on purpose

Each period reflects at the angle the Bragg law gives for its own thickness (Chapter 6: The Bragg law with refraction). In a periodic stack all the periods reflect at the same angles. Their reflections add up to sharp orders. When the period changes with depth, each period puts its orders at a slightly different angle. The orders become wider and lower. A longer period reflects at a smaller angle, so the orders spread toward the low-angle side.

Figure 22-1 shows the three cases. The Mo/Si mirror repeats one period. Its orders are sharp to 2θ = 10.5°. The W/BN mirror is lopsided: each order has a shoulder on its low-angle side. The Sb/B4C supermirror has a band at 2θ = 1.5–1.8° where a periodic stack would have a single first order.

A drift (Chapter 7: A stack that drifts) and a designed gradient look the same to the curve. The difference is in what you expect. For a drift, Chapter 17: Two ways to describe a stack asks whether the stack drifts at all. For a designed gradient you know it does, and you know roughly how.

Beyond the procedure

The fitting procedure of this book separates a periodic stack from one that drifts (§9). It has no rules for a stack graded on purpose. Its limits were set on periodic stacks. This chapter follows the X-Ray Calc Help, the paper on X-Ray Calc 3 and the program’s code, and fits the two examples as they come.

Remember: no tested limits here

The checks of Chapter 18 still work: χ², the edge, the residual by band, the parameters near a limit (Chapter 18: The residual by band). Their limits were set on periodic stacks, so treat them as guides. The order table needs separate orders. A supermirror has none.

Three ways to describe a graded stack

X-Ray Calc offers three ways to describe a stack whose layers change with depth. They differ in the number of free parameters. Every free parameter has to be fixed by something in the curve (Chapter 13: Why check before you fit). Table 22-1 compares them.

In Polynomial mode a parameter follows a polynomial in the period number, counted from the surface (Chapter 17: Two ways to describe a stack). An order-2 polynomial for the thickness of a layer costs two parameters more than a single value. In Irregular mode, every unpaired parameter has its own value in every period. A ticked box after a field pairs that parameter: it keeps one value in all the periods. Pairing is the only way to hold the count down in Irregular mode.

A gradient in Polynomial mode

Start from what you know. If the gradient is designed, enter the design before the fit. A Function profile holds a polynomial for one parameter of one layer. The fit starts its higher orders from any enabled Function profile. It starts the value at the surface, C₀, from the layer’s field.

In X-Ray Calc

Click the Add extension button in the Project panel’s toolbar. In Select type of new extension, choose Function. The Profile function editor asks for the Stack, the Layer, the parameter under Apply to (H, Sigma or Rho), the Order (1 to 9) and the coefficients c1, c2, …; Preview draws the profile. Project > New extension does the same.

Set the limits for the whole profile. A coefficient has no limits of its own. The program evaluates the polynomial over all N periods and keeps the whole profile inside the parameter’s Min and Max. When a profile leaves the window, the highest coefficients are set to zero, one at a time, until it fits. So the window in the Fitting Limits dialog is the range the gradient may cover. Make it wide enough for the whole design.

Keep the order low. The Order field takes 1 to 9. A larger number is read as 9. The Help advises 5 or less. Raise the order one step at a time, as Chapter 17: Raising the order does, and stop when χ² stops falling.

The W/BN mirror

The W/BN mirror is the Help’s example of a Polynomial fit (Examples → Polynomial Fitting on Graded PMMs). It was designed as 60 periods of W and BN on a W underlayer, under a BN cap. Its gradient was not designed. The laboratory record of the deposition says: “Deposition stuck after 36 pairs. Manual BN deposition ~ 240 sec.” The model in the paper has 60 periods. So this chapter fits both counts.

The model is the one in the paper’s project: a W underlayer, the W/BN stack, a BN cap and a C layer on top. It is started from the project’s values, with the project’s limits for H and σ. The densities are limited by the book’s rule, 0.7–1.0 of the bulk value in the program’s table (Chapter 15: The density ceiling). Every model was run five times. Table 22-2 gives the results.

The period grows toward the substrate. From order 2 on, every model agrees: about 39.5 Å at the surface and 42.0 Å next to the underlayer. The runs agree to 0.1 Å or better. Order 3 lowers χ² a little further. Orders 4 and 5, in trial runs, did no better than order 3. None of them moves the profile, so order 2 is enough. The straight line of order 1 is not. With 36 periods it even gets the direction wrong.

The curve prefers 36 periods. Every model fits better with 36 periods than with 60: χ² 3.10 against 3.98 at order 2. That agrees with the deposition record. The model in the paper has 60.

Profiling σ adds nothing here. With σ on a polynomial as well, χ² stays where it was. With 36 periods the period at each end then spreads by 0.5 to 1.2 Å over the runs. The curve can’t hold both profiles, so σ stays paired.

The shoulder stays. Figure 22-2 shows what the best model misses. Each order has a shoulder that reaches about 10 % lower in angle than its peak. By the Bragg law, that takes periods up to about 10 % longer than the period at the surface. The fitted profile grows by 6 %. None of the models found them. Two stacks, each with its own period, did worse (χ² 5.7 to 7.8). Orders 4 and 5 did no better than order 2. The model is too simple for this part of the curve (Chapter 19: The model is too simple). Report the period profile. Say that the shoulder is not reproduced.

Some runs get lost. Four of the 50 runs ended at more than twice the best χ², one of them at 227.6. Polynomial mode can send a run off in the wrong direction. Run every fit five times and set the lost ones aside (Chapter 16: Several runs, and their spread).

Two limits hold. In every run the BN density ended at 2.10 g/cm³, the bulk density in the program’s table. Treat it as a density on its ceiling (Chapter 20: A density on its ceiling). The top layers also end near their limits. The near-bound rule (Chapter 15: The near-bound rule) was tried: after four rounds of widening, the underlayer and the top layers still ended on their new limits. So the limits of the project were kept, and the layers above and below the stack are reported as undetermined.

A supermirror in Irregular mode

The Sb/B4C supermirror is the Help’s example of an Irregular fit (Examples → Supermirror). It has 50 periods of a mixed Sb2B4C layer, Sb and B4C, on glass, under a B4C layer and a C layer. The author’s project pairs the whole mixed layer, the densities of Sb and B4C and the σ of B4C. The thicknesses of Sb and B4C are free in every period. So is the σ of Sb. That makes 162 free parameters.

In X-Ray Calc

Choose Irregular under Mode in the Fitting panel. Tick the box after each field that should keep one value in all the periods. The boxes work only in a stack with N > 1. Smooth, in the same panel, works only in Irregular mode. Its window is the Smoothing window in Advanced Settings: −1 lets the program choose.

Warning: Irregular fits in 3.9.4.1130 and earlier

In X-Ray Calc 3.9.4.1130 and earlier, an Irregular fit loses its pairing at the first Shake. From then on the search barely moves. The fix is in 3.9.4.1150. The fits of this section were made with the program’s engine after the fix. Repeat any Irregular fit made with an earlier build.

A start value on a limit. The project’s stack values sit on their lower limits, the Sb and B4C thicknesses among them. Click Run in the Fitting Limits dialog. A Warning then lists each one and asks “Proceed anyway?”. With SeedR ticked, the swarm starts spread over the whole window, so the start value matters less. Answer Yes only when that is what you want.

Five runs, Smooth off. The fit started from the stack’s values, with SeedR on, and took about 30 s a run. The runs ended at χ² 5.19, 5.66, 5.81, 6.52 and 7.07. The author’s fit in the paper’s project scores 7.74. Every run beat it. Each found a different minimum.

Five runs, Smooth on. They ended at χ² 11.2 to 16.7. Smooth keeps each profile smooth from period to period during the search. It pulls the periods toward each other: their standard deviation fell to 2.8–7.4 Å, against 6.8–7.9 Å with Smooth off. The band needs the spread. Figure 22-5 shows the result. With Smooth off, the fit follows the band and the structure beyond it. With Smooth on, it keeps the outline of the band and misses the structure between 2θ = 2 and 3.5°.

Warning: Smooth assumes a smooth stack

Tick Smooth only when the design changes smoothly from period to period. A supermirror designed as a sequence of different periods is not smooth. Smooth holds the fit away from it.

Where the result goes. After an Irregular fit, the structure panel shows the values of the top period. The program keeps every period in a Table item under the model, and the Thickness page draws them. From 3.9.4.1150, the GUI starts the next Irregular fit from that Table. In 3.9.4.1130 and earlier, a new Auto Fitting, or a Resume, starts every period again from the single values in the structure panel.

Warning: continuing an Irregular fit

Before a new fit, the program asks about the extensions of the previous fit. Its Keep them option says “Use them as the starting point”. From 3.9.4.1150, Keep them, Resume and a reopened fitted project start every period from its Table value. Clear them removes the Table, and the fit starts from the single values. A Table value outside the fit limits starts from the limit, and the program asks first. After a Resume, whose limits are centered on the top period, many periods can be outside. In 3.9.4.1130 and earlier, an Irregular fit starts from the stack’s values whatever you choose. Save the project before you run again in those builds, or the periods of the last fit are lost.

What the fit can tell you about each layer

The Help says of this supermirror: “The fitting revealed the designed depth-graded structure.” Five runs of the same fit don’t agree on it. Figure 22-4 plots the period of each of the 50 periods for the five runs and for the author’s fit. In depth order, the lines cross each other everywhere. Over the five runs, the thickness of a layer in a given period spreads by about 5 Å, and by never less than 1.4 Å. The layers are 10 to 30 Å thick. By the rule of Chapter 18: What the runs don’t agree on, no single layer’s thickness is a result.

The set of periods is fixed. Sort the periods of each run by size, and the runs agree (Figure 22-4, right). In every run, and in the author’s fit, the mean period is 52 to 55 Å. The standard deviation of the periods is 6.8 to 8.0 Å. One period in ten is shorter than 42 to 46 Å. One in ten is longer than 61 to 66 Å. The total thickness of the stack agrees to about 7 %: 2586 to 2772 Å. These numbers set the band, so the curve holds them.

Runs that share a start agree with it. Five more runs started from the author’s table, period by period. They ended at χ² 6.44 to 6.86. Each period stayed near its start: the median distance from the author’s value was 0.35 Å. The runs then agree to about 1 Å in every period. That agreement comes from the start. The runs from the stack’s values reached lower χ² with other profiles.

So report what the runs agree on: the range and the mean of the periods, and the total thickness. Give the design next to them. Give a profile in depth only when runs from different starts agree on it, as they did for the W/BN mirror.

Tip: compare with the design

If you know the design, calculate its curve and compare it with the measurement before you fit. If it reproduces the band, it is a good start for Irregular mode. Runs started from it will stay near it, so their agreement doesn’t confirm it. Run the fit from the stack’s values as well.

What this means when you fit

  • Know what kind of stack you have. Sharp orders: periodic. Lopsided orders: a period that changes with depth. A band: a supermirror.
  • Choose the description with the fewest parameters that the curve needs. One period, then a polynomial, then every period on its own (Table 22-1).
  • In Polynomial mode, enter a designed gradient as a Function profile, and set Min and Max wide enough for the whole profile.
  • Raise the order one step at a time, and stop when the profile stops moving.
  • In Irregular mode, pair every parameter that shouldn’t change with depth. Leave Smooth off unless the design is smooth.
  • Save the project after an Irregular fit. From 3.9.4.1150, the next run starts from its Table unless you choose Clear them. In 3.9.4.1130 and earlier, a new run starts from the stack’s values and ignores the Table.
  • Repeat Irregular fits made with 3.9.4.1130 or earlier. They lose their pairing at the first Shake.
  • Run every fit five times, from different starts. Report what the runs agree on: a profile in Polynomial mode, the set of periods and the total in Irregular mode.
  • Say what the model misses, such as the shoulder of the W/BN orders.
