Part 3 · Chapter 15

Choosing What to Fit: Free Parameters and Limits

In This Chapter

  • Opening the Fitting Limits dialog, and what it shows
  • Setting generous limits, and why Initialize is only a start
  • The one limit with a physical meaning: density at its bulk value
  • The near-bound rule: extend the limit and fit again
  • Holding a parameter with Freeze

Every fit searches between limits. Each free parameter has a lowest and a highest value, and X-Ray Calc never looks outside them. This chapter sets the limits for the Co/C multilayer CoC4. It uses the starting model of Chapter 12: The model as text and the data prepared in Chapter 14: What this means when you fit. Then it runs the fits of Chapter 14 again with one limit moved.

What is free, and what the limits are for

X-Ray Calc fits a parameter when its Min and Max differ and it is not frozen. The fitting procedure frees the thickness H, the roughness σ and the density ρ of every layer, the contamination layer included. Two things stay as you entered them. The substrate is never fitted (Chapter 12: The substrate). Periodic mode keeps the period of the starting model (Chapter 11: Putting the period into the model). The fits of Chapters 14 to 16 hold it. Free period ± frees it (Chapter 17: Step 1, one period with the period free). For CoC4 that leaves nine free parameters: three for the contamination layer, three for the C layer of the period and three for the Co layer.

With SeedR checked, the limits are the whole search space (Chapter 9: Where the swarm starts). They don’t describe your sample. Their job is to keep the swarm away from the edges. The author of the fitting procedure put it this way: “The fitting limits must be generous: LFPSO works badly if it approaches the boundary.” A particle that crosses a limit is sent back into the range at half its speed (Chapter 9: How a particle moves). One limit is different. The upper limit of the density is the only one that states a physical fact, the density of the bulk material. The density ceiling treats it.

The Fitting Limits dialog

The dialog opens in two ways:

  • The Limits button at the top of the structure panel opens it at any time. Its action button then reads Save. Save stores the limits in the model. Cancel drops your changes.
  • Calc > Auto Fitting (F7) opens it before every fit, with the action button Run. Calc > Resume Fitting opens it with Resume. Cancel starts no fit.

Each row is a layer, under the name of its stack, from the surface down. The substrate has no row. Each parameter has three columns: Fix and its two limits. The limits are Hmin and Hmax for the thickness, Smin and Smax for σ, and RMin and RMax for ρ. The dialog doesn’t show the values themselves. Read those in the structure panel. To change a limit, click its cell, type the number and press Enter.

X-Ray Calc checks the limits as you type. A red cell has an error: its Min is larger than its Max, or its Min is negative. Run, Resume and Save then list the errors and do nothing else. A yellow cell has a warning:

  • the value lies outside its limits, or within 5 % of the range from one of them;
  • Max is more than 100 times Min, or the range is narrower than 1 % of the value;
  • RMax is above 23 g/cm³, the density of osmium, the densest stable element.

With yellow cells, Run lists the warnings and asks “Proceed anyway?”. It asks the same when no parameter is free.

Generous limits

One laboratory’s preset sets the limits this way:

  • Thickness: from two thirds to four thirds of the start value.
  • σ: 1–8 Å.
  • ρ: from the material’s own entry where the preset has one: C 1.5–2.4 g/cm³ and Co 6.0–8.79 g/cm³. Any other material runs from 0.7 to 1.0 times the bulk density of its table.
  • The contamination layer: H 5–40 Å, σ 1–10 Å, ρ 0.6–1.4 g/cm³.

Every start value lies inside its limits. The σ limits were tested on curves calculated from a known structure. A true σ of 7.5 Å, 0.5 Å from the upper limit, was found within 0.22 Å. Widening the limits to 0.5–12 Å changed nothing. The author’s own choice, generous limits with the density capped at bulk, was also tested against known structures. It held.

Initialize fills the dialog quickly. For every parameter that is not frozen, it sets Min = V(1 − Δ) and Max = V(1 + Δ). V is the value. Δ comes from the ΔH, Δσ or Δρ box, 0.25 by default. A value you pinned with Min = Max gets a window too. A density of 0 is first set to the bulk density of the table. For ρ it never puts RMax above the bulk density of the material’s table, unless your value is already above it. Table 15-1 compares the result for CoC4 with the laboratory’s limits.

In X-Ray Calc

Narrow halves every window toward the current value. Widen moves a limit out by half the range when the value lies within 5 % of the range from it. It doesn’t move an RMax at the bulk density past bulk. Fix first repairs the errors. It swaps a Min and a Max that are the wrong way round. It moves a limit out to a value that lies outside it. A density above an RMax held at bulk is the exception. Fix lowers that density itself to the bulk value. Then it does what Widen does. After Initialize, Narrow, Widen and Fix, X-Ray Calc also applies three physical rules. No Min goes below 0. No RMax goes above 23 g/cm³. No Smax is larger than the layer’s own thickness or the thickness of the layer above it. In a periodic stack, the layer above the first layer is the last layer of the period.

The density ceiling

The upper limit of ρ is the one limit with a physical meaning. In the author’s words, “The only meaningful thing is the upper limit of density”. Set RMax to the bulk density of the material’s table (Chapter 4: Where δ and β come from). For Co that is 8.79 g/cm³. Initialize puts it there for you.

Raise the ceiling only with a stated reason, one that says why your material, as you deposit it, is denser than the table. The laboratory’s preset has one such reason: sputtered carbon reaches 2.4 g/cm³, above the table’s 2.266. So its C ceiling is 2.4. The previous version of the procedure had Co at 8.9 g/cm³, above the table’s 8.79, with no reason. The current version corrected it.

A density that ends on its ceiling is reported at the ceiling. Before you read it as a dense layer, check the conditioning of the data. A wrong scale and a smoothing pass both push the density of a heavy layer up (Chapter 14: Letting the fit solve the scale). In Table 14-2 the scale held at the anchor gave Co a density 0.49 g/cm³ higher than the solved scale did.

The demo of Chapter 3: What the fit found shows what a ceiling above bulk does. Its Si limit was 2.40 g/cm³, above the table’s 2.332. The fit put the top Si density on 2.40. The true value was 2.33.

Warning: a raised ceiling moves

X-Ray Calc protects a ceiling only when RMax is at or below the bulk density of the table, as the dialog shows it to two decimals. Widen, Fix and Resume then keep it. A ceiling you raise above bulk, such as the laboratory’s 2.4 g/cm³ for C, is treated like any other limit. Widen and Fix move it out by half the range when the value lies within 5 % of the range from it: 2.4 + 0.9 / 2 = 2.85 g/cm³. Resume slides every window so that the current value sits at its center, with the same width. C at 2.00 g/cm³ with 1.5–2.4 g/cm³ becomes 1.55–2.45 g/cm³. None of them says so. The dialog opens before every Run and Resume. Check RMax there and type the ceiling back.

The near-bound rule

A fitted value that ends on a limit was set by the limit. The data had no part in it. Chapter 3 met this with a density, and Chapter 9: Random seeds met it with one run of five. The procedure’s rule:

  • A value counts when it ends on a limit, or within 5 % of the range from it.
  • Extend that limit in that direction and fit again.
  • One run is enough. If one run of the set reaches a limit, the limit has shaped the search.
  • Repeat until no free parameter of any run ends on or near a limit.
  • Report every extension you made.

The rule holds for every free parameter except the density ceiling. A density at its lower limit is extended too. After the extension, a low density of a thin metal layer may point to intermixing at its interfaces. The author’s words were “it might indicate intermixing.” Chapter 20: A low density reads such a density. The previous version of the procedure extended only H, σ and the period, and left a density at a limit alone.

X-Ray Calc uses the same 5 %. It colors such a value yellow in the Fitting Limits dialog, and Widen acts on it. For σ with limits of 1–8 Å, 5 % of the range is 0.35 Å. A fitted σ below 1.35 Å or above 7.65 Å counts.

  1. After each fit, choose Result > Fit report …. Its group Parameters within 5 % of a bound lists every free value on a limit or near it (Chapter 18: Near a limit). The Fitting Limits dialog shows the same cells in yellow.
  2. Choose Calc > Auto Fitting (F7). The Fitting Limits dialog opens with your limits as they are. Calc > Resume Fitting would move every window, as the warning in The density ceiling explains.
  3. Click Widen, or type the new limit.
  4. Check that RMax is still at the ceiling. Click Run.

The σ now sits on 0, and no σ can be lower. This limit can’t be extended. Report the σ at 0 and say that the curve sets no lower value. Do this only after checking that the other parameters did not move when the limit was extended, as here. The curve then can’t tell the outer surface of the contamination layer from a sharp one.

Freezing a parameter

A frozen parameter keeps its value in the next fit. Its limits stay as you set them, and Thaw gives them back. Setting Min = Max also pins a value, but the window is lost. The value then stays pinned until you type new limits or click Initialize. A limit left out of the model text pins the value in the same way (Chapter 12: The model as text).

There are four ways to freeze:

  • Click a cell in a Fix column. An X marks it, and its limits turn gray. Click it again to thaw.
  • Select one or more rows and click Freeze or Thaw. This acts on every parameter of those layers.
  • Right-click in the list for Freeze this stack, Freeze this layer, Freeze all and Thaw all.
  • In the structure panel, right-click a stack and choose Freeze stack or Thaw stack. Its title then shows [frozen], or a count such as [3/6 frozen].

The project saves what is frozen. Initialize, Narrow, Widen and Fix leave a frozen parameter alone. Its cells never turn red or yellow. Every parameter is free in the fitting procedure, so the procedure never freezes one. Freeze a value when you know it from another measurement. X-Ray Calc also supports fits in stages: fit the periodic stack, freeze it, then fit the rest. A frozen value is your input. It is not a fitted result.

Remember

The Fix button is not the Fix column. The button repairs and widens limits. It freezes nothing.

What this means when you fit

Free H, σ and ρ of every layer, the contamination layer included. The substrate stays as you entered it. So does the period in Periodic mode, unless Free period ± is ticked.

Set generous limits, with every start value inside. Use Initialize for the thicknesses only, then type σ and ρ.

Put RMax at the bulk density, unless you have a stated reason. A ceiling you raise above bulk can move, so check it before every Run and Resume.

After every fit, compare each value with its limits. Extend any limit that a run ended on or within 5 % of, and fit again. Report every extension.

Then choose the fit settings and the number of runs (Chapter 16: Fit Settings). Chapter 18: Judging a Fit checks the result. Chapter 20: Density explains why a fitted density is never a property of the material.
